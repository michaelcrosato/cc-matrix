    About & Credits

    Gravity Kestrel is an original spiritual successor to Starfox 64 for N64, built as a playable vertical slice. Three stages (Plains of Corvex, Ember Asteroid Belt, Gravity Core) plus a boss, lock-on missiles, smart bombs, barrel roll, boost and brake, combo scoring, and an attract-mode demo that starts after 10 seconds on the title screen.

    Controls

    • Controller: left stick fly · A laser (hold) · Y or RB missiles (lock-on fires at locked targets) · X smart bomb · B barrel roll · RT boost · LT brake · Start pause
    • Keyboard: arrows or WASD fly · Space or J laser · K missiles · B bomb · Q or E barrel roll · Shift boost · Ctrl brake · Esc or P pause
    • Settings: invert horizontal and vertical stick, screen shake on or off (saved on this device)

    Engine Feedback

    • What did you like most about the engine? The Three.js scene graph and built-in shadows, fog and tone mapping; no build step needed with an import map.
    • Did you get stuck or guess wrong? Ship nose and enemy facing conventions (ship nose is -z, enemies face +z); shadow camera bounds for a moving ship.
    • What was the most important thing you had to build yourself? Lock-on, the attract AI, the wave scheduler, the scrolling terrain height model, and unified keyboard + gamepad input with edge detection.
    • What was the hardest part about using the engine? No physics or collision helpers, so distance checks were hand-rolled; keeping pooled effects cheap without a profiler.
    • If you had to remove or overhaul one thing from the engine, what would that be? The lack of a built-in input abstraction; a gamepad helper with edge events would save time.
    • If you could change one thing about the engine, what would it be? Ship-in-scene helpers (orientation and bounds) so game-space code is less error-prone.
