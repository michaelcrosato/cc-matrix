# Air-Sea Battle (Atari, 1977) — research

Research date: 2026-11-05. The game is a fixed-screen arcade target shooter. Original designer Larry Kaplan confirms that Atari’s Anti-Aircraft coin-op inspired it, and that Sears requested the shooting-gallery dogs become rabbits. [Designer interview](https://www.ataricompendium.com/archives/interviews/larry_kaplan/interview_larry_kaplan.html).

## Confirmed mechanics and content

The [original Atari instructions](https://www.atariage.com/2600/manuals_old/air_sea_battle.html) and [scanned manual](https://atarionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Air-Sea-Battle-1977-Atari-2600-manual.pdf) describe 27 variations, rather than a campaign:

- Anti-Aircraft, 1–6: stationary cannon, 30°/60°/90° aiming; straight or guided missiles. Aircraft cross horizontally.
- Torpedo, 7–12: submarine moves across its half of the screen and fires upward; straight or guided torpedoes.
- Shooting Gallery, 13–15: cannon moves and aims; rabbits, ducks, and clowns reverse direction.
- Polaris, 16–18: moving ship shoots passing aircraft.
- Bomber, 19–21: moving aircraft drops bombs onto ships.
- Polaris vs. Bomber, 22–27: direct plane-versus-ship duel, optionally with obstructing mines.

Rounds last 2:16 or end at 99 points. Scores flash during the last 16 seconds. Difficulty changes projectile size. Each family includes computer fire variants. Some moving-craft modes preserve launch momentum; guided versions change projectile speed with craft speed.

Advanced air targets: small jet 4, large jet 3, helicopter 2, 747 1. Blimps obstruct shots without scoring. Naval targets: PT boat 4, carrier 3, pirate ship 2, freighter 1; mines obstruct without scoring. Basic variants award one point per target.

## Look, sound, tone

Gameplay screenshots show a fixed side view, horizontal sky/sea color bands, small flat single-color silhouettes, contrasting player colors, and large two-digit scores. The image is deliberately spare; it is not a first-person flight simulation. [Atari Compendium screenshots beside the designer interview](https://www.ataricompendium.com/archives/interviews/larry_kaplan/interview_larry_kaplan.html), [screenshot example](https://i0.wp.com/www.atariage.com/2600/screenshots/s_AirSeaBattle_4.png?w=1080).

Sound: a contemporary retrospective review specifically reports distinct player hit sounds and target explosion effects. Exact original waveform values and soundtrack details are unknown; no original music is claimed. [GameFAQs review](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/atari2600/584548-air-sea-battle/reviews/164722).

The instruction voice is short, practical, and inviting: commands, target values, and a friendly challenge to beat the opponent. The 1978 Atari catalogue uses a smiling cartoon naval officer and the heading “Man the Torpedoes.” [Original catalogue scan](https://www.atarimania.com/catalogues/hi_res/cat039010.jpg).

## Adaptation recommendations (new design, not historical claims)

Use an orthographic side-view 3D diorama. Preserve prediction, crossing lanes, quick target recognition, and obstructed lines of fire. Present one coherent 136-second coastal operation, with air, sea, and combined waves; keep point totals and the final countdown prominent. A colorful toy-like cel/ink treatment can extend the original silhouettes and playful catalogue tone. Guided special missiles can modernize the original guided-shot variants. Keep fire unlimited but use readable heat/cooldown feedback. Add local/online co-op and an intelligent attract player. Use new synthesised hits, launch whistles, sonar and percussion, with original quiet music and gibberish radio chatter.

Further historical context: [Atari Archive](https://www.atariarchive.org/blog/air-sea-battle-target-fun-september-1977/) explains the moving-shot variants, limited original computer opponent, and score-stealing appeal. Its named primary sources include an interview with Kaplan.
