# Source research

Inspected on 2026-11-06. Source: **Attack of the Timelord!**, North American Odyssey² release, 1982. This adaptation uses neither Doctor Who lore nor the European licensed reskin.

## References actually inspected

1. [North American instruction manual, 12-page PDF](https://odyssey2.info/library/manuals/us_timelord/Attack%20of%20the%20Timelord%20(USA).pdf). Read its extracted text, including controls, weapon introductions, progression, scoring, and Voice description. The machine moves horizontally at the bottom and has unlimited firing energy. Fleet destruction advances the level. Missiles, mines, Annihilators, and predictive Time Killers enter cumulatively. Contact destroys the original machine and restarts at level one; the manual advertises 256 increasingly difficult levels. Weapon/fleet scores are 2/4/5/8/16. High scores accept up to six letters. The optional Voice module accompanies the tyrant's reactions. The manual credits E. Averett for the software.

2. [William Cassidy's hands-on review](https://odyssey2.info/reviews/attack-of-the-timelord-6). Read the full review. It describes snaking fleets, a multicolored rotating rift, one fast player projectile at a time, a red hostile face, and mechanical synthesized speech. It distinguishes white downward darts, red seeking spheres, green crosses that roll after descending, and bright predictive diamonds; grounded crosses cannot be shot. These are behavioral descriptions, not measured steering or timing data.

3. [Gameplay screenshot](https://odyssey2.info/i/screens/time-scr.gif). Downloaded and **visually inspected**, retained as `reference/time-scr.gif` and a viewing PNG. Visible: a curved line of flat blue ship silhouettes, a pink diamond, a green cross, a triangular outlined cannon at the bottom, a mostly black field, and a magenta-bordered score/name strip. This still image does not establish animation speed or show the portal.

4. [North American box illustration](https://odyssey2.info/i/boxes/us_attack-of-the-timelord-f.webp). Downloaded and **visually inspected**, retained as `reference/box.webp`. Visible: curling blue saucer fleets surrounding a large green wireframe face, bright eyes, colored geometric weapons, and radiating rainbow lines. The cover is illustration, not a literal screenshot or proof of in-game geometry.

## Interpretation

The adaptation preserves the relationship between a horizontal defense lane, vertical interception, curling fleets, and cumulatively combined threats. It interprets the theatrical menace through an original petty tyrant and newly written subtitles. Its three integrity pips, shared slowdown meter, eight authored waves, three-phase boss, simultaneous cooperative play, and complete results flow are **new design choices**. All production art and all audio are original procedural code; downloaded references are research material only and are not rendered by the game.

## Explicit uncertainties

No gameplay video was watched. Exact original movement equations, steering limits, projectile speeds, timing intervals, and a continuous original musical score are unconfirmed. The review's gameplay descriptions were not independently measured on original hardware. The procedural score is newly composed, and the gibberish voice is newly synthesized rather than a recreation of Voice-module samples or phoneme data. Signal tests establish audible numerical output, not subjective sound quality.
