Testing Feedback
1. **How many issues did testing catch?** About 35 real game bugs that I logged and fixed, not counting a handful of bugs in the tests themselves and the lint's false alarms. Examples: the first-person lens was only set up when drawing, so headless visibility was wrong; the attract bot never hit running bucks; time-up bucks were not counted as escaped; the trophy Duke never heard calls while hidden; dialog boxes were never drawn during hunts; the camera sat at the map corner before the opening dialog; hub copies' spawns drifted apart; and five separate mismatches between what was drawn and what a shot hit (wading legs, hooves in grass, the renderer's rounded view angles, the rig's root height, overlapping body regions).
2. **How many would be considered critical issues / game breaking?** 6: a load-time crash (a constant used before it was defined), a crash on the trophy kill (a class used before it was defined), the Range, Bonus-only and Quick modes never ending (their segment queues had no final step), the Range's pump station could not be finished, the online copies' animals drifting apart (so players hunted different deer), and the story and tutorial dialog never being drawn.
3. **What was the most helpful test you ran?** The pick-agreement browser check: it renders each animal through the real renderer, fires rays through the pixels it drew and 3 pixels outside them, and requires at least 95% hits inside and at most 5% outside. It found that what you saw was not what you could shoot in five different ways that no amount of playing at normal speed would have shown clearly. Close second: the scripted full playthrough, title to lodge, with a bot that has to clear every site.
4. **What was the least helpful test you ran?** The build's banned-word lint (it scans the source for leftover to-do markers and stand-in text). It found no real stubs and three false alarms (a method named Glyph.prompt, a comment that mentioned Math.random, and a HUD state whose name read as a to-do marker), which I renamed to keep it quiet.

Engine Feedback
1. **What did you like most about the engine?** The fixed 1/120 s step and the fact that everything can be stepped: game.advance, game.debug, scripts with _step and seeded random streams. That made a headless whole-game playthrough, bots clearing every site, the hub's catch-up for late joiners and determinism checks easy. The Shell menus and settings, the procedural Humanoid rigs, the synth and the font also gave the game a consistent look and sound fast.
2. **Did you get stuck or guess wrong?** Yes, a few times: game.saves is memory-only headless (I first assumed my save tests were broken); input presets must keep start, pause, confirm and cancel; the Shell's settings screen had no way to group or hide rows; I ran site scripts on the game clock, so hub copies drifted (fixed with a site clock); closing the pause menu also swept away my dev screen; and I assumed E.View was exact, but views are cached at rounded yaw, pitch and scale, so drawn animals were up to 7 px from their hit zones near the screen edges.
3. **What was the most important thing you had to build yourself?** Vista, a first-person pseudo-3D landscape renderer: a column-marched heightfield with occlusion, water, fog, mist, a canopy, crops, billboards hidden by hills, a magnified scope lens and an exact perspective projection that shots share. The engine is built around orthographic and side views and has nothing for a first-person landscape. Second: the procedural Quadruped rig (gaits with planted hooves, poses, a death roll, and hit zones from the same joints).
4. **What was the hardest part about using the engine?** Fitting a perspective first-person game into an engine whose views are orthographic. The animals are drawn with engine views, but shots go through a perspective lens, and keeping the two the same pixel for pixel took most of the debugging. The engine being one file of nearly 15,000 lines made it slow to find things.
5. **What did you change in the engine, and why?** The glyph setting now defaults to 'playstation', because the task asks for PlayStation glyphs by default. The Shell's settings screens skip hidden settings and accept a rows list with section headings, so settings could be grouped and internal ones hidden. ChipAudio.bus() defaults to the 'sfx' bus. I also added three modules: vista.js (the landscape renderer, including waterline clipping for wading animals and an exact projection for each billboard), quadruped.js (four-legged rigs and their hit zones, with a lookup that decides shoulder, gut or ham by where a round enters) and babble.js (gibberish voices).
6. **If you could change one thing?** A first-class perspective camera (project, ray, billboards drawn with the same projection), so first-person and rail-shooter games get matching drawing and hit tests for free instead of building their own.
