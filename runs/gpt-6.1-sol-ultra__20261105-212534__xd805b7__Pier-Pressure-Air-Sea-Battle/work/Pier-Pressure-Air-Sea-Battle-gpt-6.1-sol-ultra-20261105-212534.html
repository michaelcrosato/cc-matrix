Testing Feedback

Issues caught:12 tracked issue groups were caught and fixed: AI Hub metadata, multitouch ownership, rotor timing, initial controller focus, guest disconnect recovery, mobile title clipping, dedicated-server seat routing, guest broadside routing, guest pause/menu state, touch-button visibility, horizontal-left controller aim and frame-rate-dependent physics stepping.

Critical issues:3 critical issues: two multiplayer session-edge blockers (disconnect recovery and dedicated-server seat-zero input) and frame-rate-dependent physics stepping. All were fixed before delivery.

Most helpful test:The complete AI-driven circuit used actual Rapier collisions to clear every operation. Cadence tests verified fixed simulation timing, and the upstream AI Hub harness plus real gun-input regressions covered standard, host-leave and dedicated-server sessions.

Least helpful test:Exact document-title equality was least helpful; it rejected a valid title containing the game tagline. It was replaced with an identity-prefix check.

Engine Feedback

What worked well:Three.js WebGPURenderer, TSL materials and AnimationMixer made a coherent ink-and-cel coastal scene possible with procedural assets and native keyframe clips.

Where we got stuck:Headless WebGPU screenshots were blank despite a live simulation. Visual review used the same renderer’s WebGL 2 backend; state and protocol tests verified WebGPU. Physical-device behavior is unknown.

Built for this game:The artillery rules, wave director, ballistic aiming, controller-first menus, touch assistance, procedural sound and host-authoritative multiplayer synchronization were built for this game.

Hardest engine work:The hardest engine work was keeping animated visual targets, Rapier sensors, predictive shell aiming and replicated guest state consistent across the fixed-step simulation.

Engine changes:none; engine and physics library source were not modified.

One engine improvement:A reliable headless WebGPU capture path with clearer device-loss diagnostics would make visual verification easier.

Prompt Feedback

What worked well:The specific source, single-file deliverable, controller-first direction, code-only sound and required attract mode gave the demo a clear identity and useful completion criteria.

What was confusing:The no-internet-research rule and requirement to follow an online integration repository needed interpretation: the supplied repository was read only as an implementation contract. No source research was done.

Hardest requirement:The hardest requirement was completing and verifying solo play, local co-op, direct peer play, AI Hub lifecycle, native input switching and touch assistance in the same portable file.

Suggested change:Provide the AI Hub contract files alongside the prompt and define whether interrupted multiplayer sessions count as critical issues in the exit interview.
