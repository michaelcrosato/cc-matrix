Its multiplayer mode supports up to four players running separate missions while comparing scores, both through [AI Hub sessions](https://github.com/michaelcrosato/ai-hub/tree/main/lib/hub) and standalone rooms using a [public MQTT relay](https://www.emqx.com/en/mqtt/public-mqtt5-broker).

**Testing feedback:** Testing caught **three issues**, including **one critical multiplayer lobby bug**; all three were fixed. The most helpful test was a live two-client room test, which verified joining, synchronized launch, and score updates. The least helpful was a normal headless WebGPU screenshot, which appeared blank even for a minimal test scene; offscreen WebGPU readback allowed visual inspection. The AI Hub harness passed **24/24 checks**. Mission progression was accelerated with development controls; physical controllers and normal desktop GPU presentation were not tested.

**Engine feedback:** Three.js made the procedural world approachable, and Rapier supplied useful character collision. The initial Rapier CDN filename guess was wrong, and headless WebGPU capture required investigation. The most important custom work was the mission, combat, controller interface, and multiplayer integration; combining those with strict WebGPU initialization was the hardest part. I would overhaul the renderer’s implicit fallback behavior and add a documented strict-WebGPU option with clearer device-loss feedback.

**Prompt feedback:** The controller-first, attract-mode, and single-file requirements were the strongest parts of the prompt. The standalone multiplayer format was ambiguous, so I chose synchronized side-by-side score races. The hardest part was completing the mission and both multiplayer contexts together. The most valuable additional test would be a full playthrough with physical DualSense controllers and remote players on WebGPU-capable devices.
