# Source research ledger

Research performed 2026-11-06. Source: **Yars’ Revenge**, Atari VCS/2600, 1982; original design and programming by Howard Scott Warshaw. This is an original adaptation, not an emulation.

## Primary sources consulted

- [Atari’s original-game page and reproduced instructions](https://atari.com/pages/yarsrevenge): source objective, destructible shield cells, stationary arch and shifting rectangle, left-side Zorlon alignment, refuge protection limits, vertical wrap.
- [Original Atari instruction manual reproduced at AtariAge](https://atariage.com/2600/manuals_old/yars_revenge.html): normal versus Ultimate Yars variation rules, Qotile transformation, airborne hits, one-hit deaths, escalating progression. The original charging trigger is a bite or core touch, whereas five TRONS and left-edge summoning apply specifically to Ultimate Yars.
- [Atari’s retrospective](https://atari.com/blogs/newsroom/the-addictive-and-unique-gameplay-of-yars-revenge): game identity and action context.
- [Atari’s A Fresh Look article](https://atari.com/blogs/atari/yars-revenge-a-fresh-look): sensory atmosphere, a primary-publisher animated gameplay image, narrative history, increasing challenge.
- [Animated original-game footage hosted by Atari](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0609/3658/5381/files/yars_revenge_atari_2600_gif_02_480x480.gif?v=1667920581): downloaded 64-frame animated image and sampled frames. Observed the insect’s angular silhouette, cellular shield around the core, bright striped refuge, left-rail cannon, and overwhelming full-picture color explosion after a successful shot. The animation is short: it does not independently demonstrate every variation or a full failure/progression sequence.
- [Howard Scott Warshaw interview](https://www.digitpress.com/library/interviews/interview_howard_scott_warshaw.html): the electrical laboratory hum and desire for a full-screen explosion; the neutral strip’s abstract program-code origin; controller restart intent. No sound recording is reused.
- [Original pack-in comic](https://www.atarimania.com/documents/atari_comic_yars_revenge.pdf): opened the 12-page PDF and rendered/visually inspected its first story page (PDF page 3). Its formal military briefing, insect anatomy, bright cosmic pulp settings, and sincere science-fiction language informed tone. Its text, characters, imagery, and plot are not copied into the game.

## Verified source relationships

The original uses safe shield nibbling or ranged fire to cut a corridor. Only the remote cannon kills the Qotile or Swirl. The Yar must align vertically and escape its own firing lane. The neutral strip disables weapon commands and protects against Destroyer Missiles, but does not stop Swirls. The Qotile itself transforms into a windup/flight Swirl. Flight wraps top/bottom. A bad cannon shot or a hostile collision can kill the Yar. Later rounds alternate shield form and increase pressure.

Both shield configurations, normal cannon/core killing, airborne Swirl killing, failure and progression were checked in the instruction text. Direct visual observation is limited to the publisher’s short animated excerpt; an airborne kill, failure, and full multi-round progression were not independently observed in longplay footage. Those limitations are stated rather than presented as verified footage fidelity.

## Deliberate inventions in Yars of Ruin

Three finite authored sectors and an ending; named original stations and swarm; independent move/aim; visible multi-bite charge; simultaneous local/online co-op with a shared reserve; per-scout remote guns and friendly cannon danger; distinct reflective shield materials; a final airborne-only heart that repeatedly docks for renewable charge; contextual tutorials, Assist, practice, records, designer tools, and synthesized original music/gibberish voice. Reflective behavior is an explicitly taught adaptation, not a claim that every original variation reflected shots.
