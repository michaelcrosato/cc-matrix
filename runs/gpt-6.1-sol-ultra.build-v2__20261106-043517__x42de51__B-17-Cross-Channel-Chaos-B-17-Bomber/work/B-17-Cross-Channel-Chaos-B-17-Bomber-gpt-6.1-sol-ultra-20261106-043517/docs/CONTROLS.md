# B-17 Cross Channel Chaos — flight handbook

Destroy the armed transport at Greyhaven, then bring the crew back to Rookfield. The battery, harbor works and inland refinery are optional. A safe abort is always available through the operational map. The demo operation lasts about ten to twelve active minutes.

## Start and prepare

Open the delivered single HTML file in a browser with WebGL 2. Choose **Fly a sortie**, compare the direct and northern dogleg routes, choose Standard / Long Legs / Heavy Hand payload and Cadet / Crew / Veteran skill, then launch. Every offered loadout can complete the required strike and return. Extra bombs cost weight and fuel; optional targets never supply required resources.

For two people, choose **Co-op crew**, then keyboard/mouse plus a standard pad or two standard pads. The bomber and stores are shared. Each crew member receives a separate first-person view. The initial commander flies; the other crew member starts at the nose gun.

Prompts follow the last device. Settings can use Xbox glyphs instead, adjust dead zone/sensitivity/inversion, enlarge captions, reduce motion/shake and set independent audio volumes. The music starts at ten percent. Sound unlocks on the first deliberate interaction; inability to unlock audio never blocks the sortie.

## Fly, defend, bomb, return

The navigator initially holds the chosen route. Direct flight input takes control immediately. Increase power to climb, keep the stall warning clear, and use H/L3 when you want navigation hold again. Leaving the pilot station lets the crew stabilize flight; it does not dodge a flak danger zone for you.

When the radio names a clock bearing, take that mount: nose twelve, starboard three, tail six, port nine. Aim ahead of the moving fighter and fire short bursts. Every mount has finite ammunition and its own bounded arc. Unattended crew members provide modest suppression. A jammed mount leaves other bearings and evasion available.

At the coast, the amber world-space danger ring marks a coming flak burst. Bank or change altitude before it bursts. Destroying the optional battery reduces that local threat. The operational map keeps flight and fuel moving; previewing a destination does not commit it. The flight commander commits a course explicitly.

Enter the bombsight early. Doors open on entry; wait for **DOORS OPEN**. The impact reference includes aircraft velocity, gravity and wind. Align by course/flight and release timing, then use click/Space/R2 to release the selected finite salvo. Looking around the sight does not move a bomb's origin. A miss leaves another pass or an abort; the last bomb never prevents a return. A damaged sight retains a coarse reference.

After the transport is destroyed, choose Rookfield on the map, or assess fuel/condition before trying the optional refinery. Reaching the home approach invokes assisted recovery. The debrief shows objective/crew outcome, accuracy, fighter score, return bonus and damage/wasted-bomb deductions. Repair/rearm and fly again resets the aircraft for a new seed.

## Crew authority and touch

One human or AI owns each station. Selecting an occupied seat through station selection shows its owner; select it again to exchange duties. Taking the pilot seat explicitly transfers flight command. The commander keeps route/emergency permission while looking at a gun or bombsight. A departing crew member yields their duties to AI. Either local player can pause the shared sortie; an AI Hub overlay leaves the shared aircraft flying.

On touch devices, drag the left half to fly and the right half to aim. Routine throttle/hold are assisted. Context actions appear for the next useful station, held **FIRE BURST**, explicit bomb release, emergency fire bottle and home route. Pause → **Emergency actions** offers the bottle and a deliberate 1.5-second payload-jettison hold. The persistent menu opens the same map and screens. Touch is solo; use pads or AI Hub for co-op.

## Practice, demonstration and tools

The title's practice menu starts directional gunnery, bombing calibration or a damaged return using the real simulation. Ten idle seconds on the title starts an isolated crew demonstration; deliberate input returns to the menu and is consumed.

Pause → **Dev mode** exposes testing tools and live flight/combat/resource sliders. Reset tuning restores canonical defaults. Cheated runs are marked. Mission authoring can move a waypoint and associated target in metres, apply changes, preview volumes and import/export a validated definition. World-changing tools are disabled in a live AI Hub round; local display/audio settings remain available.

This is an original game inspired by **B-17 Bomber (Intellivision, 1982)**. The source used fuel jettison; this adaptation deliberately jettisons payload. It contains original procedural art, music, effects and invented radio voices. See About/Credits and `research.md` for sources and dependency notices.
